Google has moved most categories from postcards to live video verification. The rules are
strict, largely undocumented, and a rejection tells you nothing about which part failed.

Attempts are also limited in practice. After several rejections the option often disappears and
the profile goes to manual review. So the first recording is the one that matters.

## What the recording has to prove

A reviewer is checking three things, and all three have to be visible in **one continuous
take**:

1. **That the location exists and is where you say it is.**
2. **That the business operating there matches your chosen category.**
3. **That you personally have authority over it.**

Everything below follows from those three.

## The shot list

Record in this order. Do not stop, do not cut, do not switch apps.

**Start outside.** Film the street, then pan to the building. Get the street sign or a
recognisable landmark in frame so the location can be placed. Then get your signage with the
business name clearly legible.

**Show the address.** A number on the door, a plaque, or a piece of business mail held up at
the entrance. This is the step people skip, and it is one of the most common reasons for
rejection.

**Walk inside without stopping the recording.** The continuity between outside and inside is
part of what is being verified. Cutting here reads as two unrelated clips.

**Show the workspace and your category evidence.** A dentist films the chair and the equipment.
A restaurant films the kitchen. A locksmith films the key-cutting machine and the stock. The
reviewer is matching what they see against the category you selected — generic office footage
proves nothing.

**Prove you manage the place.** Open the till. Unlock the back office. Show the booking system
or the point-of-sale logged in. Show branded documentation, invoices or headed paper. Anything
a customer could not do.

## What gets you rejected

- **Stopping and restarting.** The take has to be unbroken. This is the number one failure.
- **No legible address anywhere in the video.** Signage with the business name is not enough on
  its own.
- **Filming a residential address on a profile that displays its location.** If you work from
  home, the profile has to be configured as a service-area business with the address hidden
  *before* you record.
- **Category mismatch.** Filming an empty room when your category implies specialised
  equipment.
- **Someone else recording it.** The person filming has to be able to demonstrate management
  authority. Sending a friend to film the shopfront does not work.
- **Poor light or shaky footage.** If the reviewer cannot read your signage, it does not count.

## Before you press record

Walk the route once without recording. Check that the signage is legible in the current light,
that you have a piece of addressed mail to hand, and that you can get from outside to your
category evidence without stopping.

Charge the phone. Record landscape. Keep it under five minutes — longer is not better, and a
long recording with the key evidence buried in it is worse than a tight three-minute take.

## Service-area and home-based businesses

If you have no premises a customer visits, the setup work comes first: the profile must be a
service-area business with the address hidden, and the service area must be one you can
plausibly cover. Then the recording shifts to your vehicle, your branded equipment, your stock
and your documentation rather than a shopfront.
