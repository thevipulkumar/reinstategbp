You logged in this morning and your listing is gone. No warning, no explanation beyond a
generic notice, and no obvious way to talk to a human about it.

The first thing worth knowing: this is almost never personal, and it is almost never random.
Google's trust system flagged something specific about your profile. Once you know what, the
route back is usually short.

## Soft suspension or hard suspension?

These are different problems and the difference matters immediately.

A **soft suspension** leaves your listing visible in Search and Maps, but you lose the ability
to manage it. Your dashboard shows the profile as suspended while customers still see it
normally. Your reviews, photos and ranking stay where they are.

A **hard suspension** removes the profile entirely. Search for your business and it is not
there. Reviews you spent years earning are gone from public view, and the listing no longer
appears in the local pack.

Check which one you have before doing anything else. A hard suspension is urgent; a soft one
gives you room to prepare a proper case.

## The triggers we see most

### Your address doesn't match how you actually trade

This is the single most common cause. A virtual office, a co-working desk, a mail-forwarding
address or a PO box will all get flagged. So will a home address on a profile configured to
display its location publicly.

Google cross-references your address against its own data about that building. If several
unrelated businesses share it, or if it resolves to a service that rents addresses, the profile
gets pulled.

### Keywords in your business name

"Miller Plumbing" is fine. "Miller Plumbing Emergency 24/7 Chicago" is a guideline violation,
and one of the fastest ways to lose a listing. Your name on Google has to match the name on
your signage, your invoices and your registration — nothing more.

This one stings because it often follows advice from a previous agency. It is still a violation.

### Editing too much at once

Changing the name, the address, the primary category and the phone number in the same week
looks exactly like a hijacked profile. Bulk edits after an ownership change trigger suspensions
constantly, and the business owner making them has no idea they are doing anything unusual.

### A category Google polices harder

Locksmiths, towing, garage door repair, addiction treatment, legal services and general home
repair are held to a stricter standard than everyone else, because those categories attract the
most listing fraud. Legitimate businesses get caught in that net every day.

### Duplicate listings

An old profile from a previous owner, a second listing an agency created without telling you,
or a practitioner listing overlapping the business listing — any of these will pull down the
profile you actually use.

## What to do in the first 24 hours

**Don't file an appeal yet.** This is the mistake that costs the most. A rejected appeal is
attached to your profile's history and makes the next one harder to win. Filing before you know
what triggered the suspension is a coin flip with a real downside.

Instead:

1. **Screenshot everything.** The suspension notice, your dashboard, your current profile
   details. You will need the exact wording later.
2. **Work out what changed.** Check your email for Google notifications about profile edits.
   Something moved shortly before the suspension — find it.
3. **Read your profile against the guidelines.** Specifically: is your name exactly your
   trading name? Does your address match where you actually work? Is your service area
   plausible?
4. **Fix the violation first, then appeal.** Appealing while the problem is still live on the
   profile guarantees a rejection.

## What happens after you appeal

Reinstatement requests are reviewed by a person, but quickly. They are looking for one thing:
does the evidence resolve the specific violation that was flagged? Not whether your business is
real, not how long you have been trading — just whether the flagged issue has been answered.

Appeals that explain how established the business is, without addressing the violation, get
closed. Appeals that address the violation directly, with documentation that proves it, get
reinstated — usually within a few business days.
